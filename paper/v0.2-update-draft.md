# Draft: updates to the paper for v0.2

Notes for the author. This is a proposal for how the paper's numbers and claims move when it is updated to v0.2.
Nothing in `main.tex` has been changed. The v0.1 experiments were ported to v0.2 as E2 (overhead), E4 (seeded faults)
and E5 (real Claude Code runs), with new scenarios and numbers (see `docs/evaluation.md`). The v0.1 scripts are in
git history; the paper's tables and figures still show the v0.1 results until it is updated.

## What changed in the system

- The log is now written by a separate signer process (a separate OS user in system mode). Each record is
  Ed25519-signed in addition to hash-chained, and signed checkpoints go to independent witnesses.
- The evidence unit is a `.tkb` bundle verified offline, with a verdict that says when it is unanchored.
- Capture sources: hooks, an optional model-API proxy, a harness-transcript cross-check, and an SDK.
- The policy gate has rule ids, `deny`/`ask`/`flag`, approvals from outside the session, and a recorded policy hash.

## Replacing the v0.1 integrity claims

The v0.1 anchoring analysis (detection of a full re-chain depends on how often the head is saved elsewhere) now maps
onto witness checkpoints, and `docs/evaluation.md` has the v0.2 numbers: 8 mutation classes, 60 trials each. The
result worth stating in the paper is the two cases the ledger cannot catch alone (tail truncation and a re-sign by a
key holder) and that a witness catches both; the residual risk is the interval after the last checkpoint.

Suggested conservative wording: "With a signing key held by a separate OS user and an off-host witness, the
evaluated mutation classes were detected in all trials. Without the witness, truncation and re-signing by a key
holder were not detected."

## Policy results

The v0.1 paper reports its gate on a corpus of 84 calls. The v0.2 default policy on the same corpus blocks 43 of 44
harmful calls and none of 40 benign ones, **after the rules were tuned on it**. That is a development-set result,
and it should be labelled as one. The rougher second set (12 of 16 blocked, 1 of 20 benign blocked) is a better
indication of behaviour on variants; the misses are listed in `docs/evaluation.md`.

## Not to claim

- Anything about real-agent behaviour beyond E5: 12 runs of one model on one small project, where the model ignored
  both planted injections, so the gate was not exercised.
- Overhead numbers from other machines: E2 was measured on one Linux host (and one Mac for the offline checks).
- That the gate stops a determined agent: it is a tripwire for obvious actions.
- Anything about macOS system mode or the Rekor witness beyond "implemented, not validated on hardware / the live service".

## Experiments worth running for a v0.2 version

More real-agent runs with planted injections that the model actually follows (E5 produced none), signer throughput
with a separate-user signer, a bypass study with an adversarial agent attempting to disable hooks or edit the transcript, and an
external review of the threat model.
